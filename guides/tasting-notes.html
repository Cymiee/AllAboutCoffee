Why You Can't Taste the Notes on Your Coffee Bag

Your bag promises blueberry and jasmine. You taste… coffee. Here's what's going on.

On this page

    What the notes on the bag actually mean

    “Blueberry, jasmine, dark chocolate” does not mean anything was added. Those are descriptors a taster reached for when comparing this coffee against others — they are comparisons, not ingredients.

    That distinction matters, because it sets a very different expectation for what you are hunting for in the cup.

    Why your brew hides them

    Tasting notes are usually established under controlled conditions: a specific ratio, grind, water and temperature. Change any of those and the delicate high notes are the first thing to disappear.

    • Stale beans — aromatics fade within weeks of roasting
    • Over-extraction — bitterness buries everything subtle
    • Water that is too hot, or heavily mineralised
    • Pre-ground coffee, which loses aromatics within minutes

    How to start noticing more

    Try the same coffee brewed two ways on the same morning and taste them side by side. Differences are far easier to detect by comparison than in isolation — which is exactly how the taster wrote those notes in the first place.

    If you taste nothing but “coffee” at first, that is normal. Palate is trained, not innate.
