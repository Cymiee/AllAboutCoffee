The Healthy Coffee Guide

Which coffees are actually good for you — and which café habits quietly undo the benefits.

On this page

    What “healthy” actually means here

    Coffee itself is not the problem. In most research, black coffee comes out neutral to mildly positive — it is what gets added to it, and how much of it you drink, that moves the needle.

    This guide is about the choices that actually change the nutrition of what is in your cup, rather than the ones that only sound healthy.

    Where the benefits come from

    Coffee carries antioxidants and polyphenols, most notably chlorogenic acid. Lighter roasts retain more of it, which is one of the few places where roast level has a measurable nutritional effect rather than just a flavour one.

    • Lighter roasts keep more chlorogenic acid
    • Filtered brewing removes cafestol, which can raise cholesterol
    • Unsweetened is doing more work than any “superfood” additive

    What quietly undoes them

    A large flavoured latte can carry more sugar than a dessert. The coffee is still coffee — the syrup, whipped cream and flavoured powder are what change the picture.

    Rough rule: the further your order gets from “coffee and milk”, the more it behaves like a soft drink.
