How to Store Coffee So It Stays Fresh

The freezer debate, settled — plus the four things that actually stale your beans.

On this page

    The four enemies of fresh coffee

    Coffee goes stale for four reasons, and none of them is time on its own. Time only matters because it gives these four a chance to act.

    • Oxygen — the main culprit; oxidises the oils
    • Moisture — begins extraction before you ever brew
    • Heat — accelerates every other process
    • Light — degrades aromatic compounds

    The freezer question

    Freezing works, with one condition: it must be genuinely airtight, and you must not thaw and refreeze. Every thaw cycle condenses moisture onto the beans, which is the one thing you were trying to avoid.

    For coffee you will finish within a few weeks, the freezer is unnecessary. For a bag you want to keep for months, freeze it in small portions you can pull out one at a time.

    What to actually do

    An opaque, airtight container at room temperature, away from the stove, and grinding only what you need for that brew. That combination beats almost any storage gadget.

    Grinding ahead of time costs you more freshness than a week of decent storage ever will.
